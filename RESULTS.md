# Results

Run 2026-10-07. 60 known-human Reddit accounts (20 comments each, all written before ChatGPT launched)
vs 120 AI accounts that replied to the **same threads** at matched lengths: one plain-prompted and one
told to "sound like a casual redditor" per human account, generated by Gemini 3.8 Flash, GPT-5.6 Luna,
Llama 4 Maverick and Qwen3-235B (30 accounts each). Every judge returns a 0-100 "AI probability".

- **AUROC**: how well the score separates AI from human accounts (1.0 perfect, 0.5 coin flip).
- **Catch@5%**: share of AI accounts flagged at a cutoff that wrongly flags at most 5% of humans.

## Whole account vs single comment (Haiku 5.5, first prompt)

| Unit | AUROC | Catch@5% | Cost / 180 |
|---|---|---|---|
| Whole account (20 comments, one call) | 0.966 | 0.83 | $0.11 |
| One comment | 0.748 | 0.38 | $0.015 |

Single-comment judging is barely useful. Everything below judges whole accounts.

## Cheap judges, behavior prompt (v2)

The v2 prompt tells the judge to ignore typography (dashes, quotes, slang, typos), which any bot can be
told to fake, and to weigh behavior: uneven effort, rounded-off takeaways, lived detail, one voice
everywhere, restating the parent.

| Judge | AUROC | Catch@5% | Cost / 180 accounts |
|---|---|---|---|
| **GPT-6 Luna, comment text only** | **0.997** | **0.98** | $0.044 |
| GPT-6 Luna, with thread context | 0.983 | 0.93 | $0.071 |
| Claude Haiku 5.5 | 0.965 | 0.75 | $0.109 |
| Qwen 3.7 Flash | 0.882 | 0.67 | $0.080 |
| gpt-oss-120b | 0.731 | 0.03 | $0.030 |
| Mistral Small 3.2 | 0.654 | 0.00 | $0.048 |
| gpt-oss-20b | 0.457 | 0.00 | $0.018 |

GPT-6 Luna scores 0.97-1.00 on bots from every generator family, not only the GPT-written ones, so the
result isn't a model recognizing its own family. Without thread context it put no human above 12/100,
52 of 60 casual-prompted bots above 20, and every plain-prompted bot above 75. DeepSeek V4 Flash was
dropped after a 6-account pilot (costlier than Haiku because of reasoning tokens, 2 of 6 replies
unparseable). Qwen 3.8 Flash and Gemma 4 did not finish.

## Blind hand check

12 randomly drawn accounts, usernames hidden, judged by hand before looking at labels: 12/12 correct,
and the Haiku judge (first prompt, cutoff 25) agreed on all 12.

## What gave AI accounts away

Not typography. Consistent competence on every comment, a polished closing takeaway, generic
"experience" that could fit anyone, one voice across unrelated subreddits, and the occasional template
leftover (`[link/name of cafe]`). Real accounts had lazy one-liners, wrong guesses, running jokes and
specific lived detail.

## Caveats

- 60 accounts per group is small; treat differences under ~0.03 AUROC as noise.
- "Human" means pre-ChatGPT; old repost bots could be among them.
- The AI side is our own prompts. Operators who actively tune against detection will do better than
  "sound casual".
- Generation needs care: reasoning models hit token limits and returned cut-off replies (75% of Gemini's
  in the first pass) or refusals, which make any judge look better. `generate_ai.py` rejects both.
- Raw data (Reddit comments and usernames) is not published; rerun the scripts to reproduce it.
